Direction A

Race instrument

A softly lit circuit maquette on a charcoal studio ground. The entire field stays visible, with precise timing immediately below.

What defines it

  • Modelled cars, bridge, grandstands and terrain
  • Mono timing and powder-blue camera selection
  • Race order remains on the main screen

The trade-off

Less playful than C, but the strongest fit for cinematic, precise, tactile spectatorship.

My recommendation: A — Race instrument.

It feels like watching a race, not playing a racing game. B is clearer editorially but less immersive; C has the strongest material identity, with a deliberate toy-like feel.

Proposed signature: hold the race, then dive into it.

Tap a car to move into a trailing 3D camera. Pinch back out to frame the entire field. One continuous world—not a collection of dashboards.

Visual concepts, not a live viewer. Stills from original 3D models; all car positions are simulated. Gesture, camera and sync controls are proposals. “Vantage” is a working name.

Data reality: OpenF1 documents paid live access; commercial use needs discussion. Free historical access does not establish a free, commercially cleared live feed.

Design checks & files

Refused: news-first dashboards, red/black broadcast clutter, neon HUDs and tiny flat track widgets. Second pass improved callout anchors, timing units, small-phone fitting and circuit symbols. Extra driver hues are semantic; type systems are intentionally different across the concepts.
